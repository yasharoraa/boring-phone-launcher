Privacy Policy — Boring Launcher

Applies to the Android app com.fieapps.minilauncher (“Boring Phone / Boring Launcher”). Last updated 26 September 2026.

The short version

Boring Launcher does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal or sensitive user data. It has no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind. Everything the app records stays in its own private storage on your device.

The app is built without the Android INTERNET permission. You can verify this yourself in the Play Store listing under “App permissions”, or by inspecting the installed package. Without that permission the app is technically incapable of sending anything anywhere.

What is stored on your device

All of the following is stored only in the app’s private storage, is never transmitted, and is excluded from Android cloud backup and device-to-device transfer.

WhatWhy
Your chosen home-screen apps, hidden apps and appearance settingsSo the launcher looks and behaves the way you set it up
Tasks and memos you write, and their reminder timesTo show them and to schedule local reminders
Which apps you marked as distracting, and any time budgetsTo show the Intentional Pause and enforce your own limits
A local log of when you opened, declined or overrode an appTo show counts such as “pauses that worked”. Entries older than 90 days are deleted automatically
Which purchases you ownSo Pro features keep working without a network connection

Permissions and what they are used for

Usage access (optional)

If you grant it, the app reads Android’s own usage statistics to show your screen time and to enforce time budgets you have set. These figures are read on the device, displayed to you, and never leave the device. If you do not grant it, the Usage screen and time budgets simply stay empty.

Notification access (optional, off by default)

The optional Notification Digest holds back notifications from apps you explicitly choose. To do this, Android must let the app see notifications as they arrive. When the feature is switched on, and only for apps you have selected, the app stores the notification’s app name, title and text in its private database and dismisses the notification so it does not interrupt you.

Notifications from apps you have not selected are ignored and never stored. Your default phone app, your default messaging app and the launcher’s own reminders are excluded in code and cannot be selected. Stored items are deleted automatically after seven days, and you can clear them at any time from the digest screen. None of this content is ever transmitted.

Device administrator (optional, off by default)

If you turn on “double-tap to lock”, Android requires the app to be registered as a device administrator, because that is the only way an app is permitted to turn off the screen. The app requests the force-lock policy and nothing else. It does not set password rules, does not erase data, does not disable the camera and does not monitor the device. You can revoke it at any time from the app’s settings; the only effect is that double-tap to lock stops working.

Other permissions

Notifications are used for your own task reminders and session timers. Exact alarms, if you allow them, make those reminders arrive on time. The boot permission re-schedules your pending reminders after a restart. The status-bar permission lets a downward swipe open the notification shade. The uninstall permission shows Android’s own uninstall dialog when you choose “Uninstall” on an app.

Purchases

Boring Launcher offers a one-time in-app purchase through Google Play Billing. Payments are processed entirely by Google; the app never sees or stores your payment details. The app only asks Google Play which products this device’s account owns, so it can unlock the features you paid for. Google’s handling of that transaction is covered by the Google Privacy Policy.

Children

This app is not directed at children. It collects no data from anyone, including children.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling the app removes everything it has stored. You can also clear individual items inside the app — delete tasks and memos, clear the digest, or use Android’s “Clear storage” option in system settings for the app. Because nothing is ever sent off the device, there is no server-side copy to request the deletion of.
